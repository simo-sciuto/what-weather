## What it says for us

- The report's claims hold in substance: Poster Armory's 10 USD a month is confirmed; TieDays' "from 59 USD" is the digital page, a poster is 79 USD more. Ondo's price was not visible.
- A digital file sits between 13 and 59 in these shops (Map & Print 12.99 GBP, TieDays 59 USD). The experimental 6,90 EUR of ADR-018 is below all of them: a price to test, not a position. A higher first test (about 12 to 15 EUR) is worth running beside it.
- Only TieDays and Ondo use weather. Neither says how the weather was obtained or how sure it is. Provenance, model and confidence on every record (ADR-016) is a real difference and costs nothing to show, but it is a claim to put in front of buyers, not proven to matter to them.
- Nobody here is also a daily weather app. The free Explore side is the other difference: it is also the traffic.
- TieDays is the closest in idea (a moment, its sky and weather) and sells the page and the story, not the poster alone. The poster is its add-on.

## Proposal: three directions for Create (to judge by eye, with the user)

The record engine already has the layouts and the palettes; the three should be three settings of it, not three new engines. Names follow the assessment.

1. **Editorial**: the classic poster as it is today (map under the veils, name in Inter Tight, facts in Schibsted Grotesk), in the page's atmosphere palette. The default.
2. **Monochrome**: one ink and the paper, the map as lines, the temperature as the only weight. Cheapest to print, and the strongest on a wall.
3. **Chromatic**: the atmosphere at full range (sky gradient, bloom, temperature colour), the most expressive and the most shareable.

Open for the user: whether these three are the right three, and whether Monochrome and Chromatic are variants of the same layouts or have their own. Nothing is built.
